This page explains the Canadian tax terms Personal Tax uses, in plain words.

## The T1 return

Each person who lives in Canada files their own income tax and benefit return for a calendar year, called the T1. It
reports their income and claims their deductions and credits. It works out federal tax and the tax of the province they
lived in on December 31.

## The filing deadline

For most people, the return and any balance owing are due on April 30 of the next year: April 30, 2026 for the 2025 tax
year. People who are self-employed, and their spouses, have until June 15 to file, but any balance is still due on
April 30.

## Slips and receipts

Employers, banks and others send slips that report income and amounts paid:

- **T4:** employment income, and the tax, CPP and EI taken off your pay.
- **T5:** interest and dividends.
- **RRSP contribution receipt:** money put into a registered retirement savings plan.
- **T4FHSA:** contributions to and withdrawals from a first home savings account.

You also keep receipts for amounts you claim, such as donations and medical expenses.

## The notice of assessment

After the CRA processes a return, it sends a notice of assessment. It confirms the result and shows amounts for the next
year, such as the RRSP deduction limit and unused amounts that can be carried forward.

## Spouses and common-law partners

You are married if you are legally married. You generally live common-law if you live with your partner in a conjugal
relationship and either have lived together for at least 12 months in a row, or have a child together. The CRA's
definition has more detail. Each spouse files their own return, and some amounts, such as donations and medical
expenses, can be claimed by either one.

## Province of residence

Provincial tax is the tax of the province you lived in on December 31, even if you lived somewhere else for part of the
year. Quebec residents also file a separate Quebec return.
