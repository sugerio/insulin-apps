This page explains the Canadian tax terms Taxi uses, in plain words.

## The T1 return

Each person who lives in Canada files their own income tax and benefit return for a calendar year, called the T1. It
reports their income and claims their deductions and credits. It works out federal tax and the tax of the province they
lived in on December 31.

## The filing deadline

For most people, the return and any balance owing are due on April 30 of the next year: April 30, 2026 for the 2025 tax
year. People who are self-employed, and their spouses, have until June 15 to file, but any balance is still due on
April 30.

## Slips and receipts

Employers, banks and others send slips that report income and amounts paid:

- **T4:** employment income, and the tax, CPP and EI taken off your pay.
- **T5:** interest and dividends.
- **RRSP contribution receipt:** money put into a registered retirement savings plan.
- **T4FHSA:** contributions to and withdrawals from a first home savings account.

You also keep receipts for amounts you claim, such as donations and medical expenses.

## The notice of assessment

After the CRA processes a return, it sends a notice of assessment. It confirms the result and shows amounts for the next
year, such as the RRSP deduction limit and unused amounts that can be carried forward.

## Spouses and common-law partners

You are married if you are legally married. You generally live common-law if you live with your partner in a conjugal
relationship and either have lived together for at least 12 months in a row, or have a child together. The CRA's
definition has more detail. For tax, the two are the same: the same credits, transfers and benefits. Each spouse files their own return, and some amounts, such as donations and medical
expenses, can be claimed by either one.

## Province of residence

Provincial tax is the tax of the province you lived in on December 31, even if you lived somewhere else for part of the
year. Quebec residents also file a separate Quebec return.

## The US federal return

This part is about the United States. A U.S. citizen or resident files Form 1040 for each calendar year with the IRS
(the Internal Revenue Service). It reports income, takes deductions and credits, and works out the federal tax. Each
state has its own return. Taxi prepares only the federal one.

- **The deadline.** For the 2025 tax year, the return and any tax you owe are due on April 15, 2026. Form 4868 gives
  you until October 15, 2026 to file, but not to pay.
- **Filing status.** Single, married filing jointly, married filing separately, head of household, or qualifying
  surviving spouse. Your status on December 31 and the people who live with you decide which ones you can use. Filing
  jointly, a married couple files one return with both incomes, and usually pays less.
- **Forms you get.** A Form W-2 from each employer, by January 31. A Form 1099-INT from a bank that paid you $10 or
  more of interest, and a Form 1099-DIV for dividends. A Form 1099-B from a broker for what you sold. A Form 1099-R for
  money from an IRA or pension, and a Form SSA-1099 for social security benefits. A Form 1099-NEC from a business that
  paid you $600 or more as a contractor. A Form 1098 for mortgage interest, a Form 1098-T from a college, and a Form
  1098-E from a lender that received $600 or more of student loan interest.
- **The standard deduction or itemizing.** Most people take the standard deduction. For 2025 it is $15,750 for a
  single person or for each spouse filing separately, $31,500 for a married couple filing jointly, and $23,625 for a
  head of household. It is larger at 65 or older and for a blind person. You itemize instead (Schedule A) when your
  mortgage interest, state and local taxes, gifts to charity and large medical expenses add up to more. If one spouse
  filing separately itemizes, the other must too.
- **Capital gains.** A gain on something you held more than a year, and qualified dividends, are taxed at 0%, 15% or
  20%, less than other income. A capital loss lowers your income by $3,000 at most in a year; the rest carries over.
- **The alternative minimum tax.** A second way of working out the tax, with fewer deductions. You pay the larger of
  the two. Few people owe it.
- **Credits.** A credit lowers the tax itself. Some are refundable: you get them even when they are more than your
  tax, such as the earned income credit and part of the child tax credit.
- **The social security number.** Each person on the return, and each dependant, needs one (or a taxpayer
  identification number). Taxi never asks for it: you write it on the paper return.
